de Souza et al., Fig. 13: a strip of ten photographs captioned as the grasp scenarios of its Table 1. The caption does not number them. Taking them left to right as scenarios 1 to 10 is our reading; Table 1's own photograph of each scenario is the picture each scenario was copied from first.

Cobos et al., Figure 5: the caption names its five photographs by class only: a) circle power grasp (tennis ball), b) circle power grasp (big ball), c) prismatic power grasp (hammer), d) circle precision grasp (coin), e) prismatic precision grasp (pen). Listing photograph c under "medium wrap" and photograph d under "thumb 1 finger" is our reading of those two pictures, not the paper's statement.

Cobos et al., Figure 4: the sub-classes recorded here (medium wrap, heavy wrap, thumb with 4, 2 and 1 fingers) appear in that paper only as labelled drawings in this figure. Its text and its experiment deal with four gestures (circular power, prismatic power, circular precision, prismatic precision) and do not describe the hand shape of a sub-class. Under several labels the figure shows three different drawings (heavy wrap, medium wrap and circular power among them); the operator had all of them on screen, and which one was copied was not recorded.
