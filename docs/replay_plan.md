# Replay reconstruction and Paper 3 comparison: implementation plan

Written 2026-10-23 before coding. The acceptance checks are the ledger
`GATES_replay.md` at the repository root. Codex verifies independently; this
plan says what was built, how it is checked and what the numbers mean.

## Two different claims, kept apart

1. **Replay fidelity**: the viewer shows what the tracker recorded. Checked
   numerically against the recording itself (the `abs26` positions the
   recorder wrote next to the parent-relative chain) and against arithmetic
   done two independent ways.
2. **Paper 3 agreement**: how the recorded angles sit against the
   intra-finger relationships of Cobos et al. 2009. A research question, not a
   fidelity question. No pass/fail threshold: the paper supplies none.

Neither is physical accuracy against the person's hand. That needs an
independent reference measurement this dataset does not have.

## Facts established from the sources (2026-10-23)

- Dataset: `grasp set 2026-10-08 organized` in the dataset workspace.
  115 static takes (`metadata/grasps_summary.csv`, 69,761 JSONL lines,
  51,620 lines of the selected hand) and 52 transition captures
  (`metadata/pose_transitions.csv`, 20,809 lines, 20,089 selected).
- Every line has `wall_time` (writer clock), `capture_time` (camera
  acquisition instant on the wall clock, 1e-6 s), `timestamp` /
  `timestamp_us` (LeapC device clock, integer microseconds), `frame_id`,
  `hand_id`, `hand_side`, `status`, the 26 parent-relative `joints`
  (translation metres + XYZW quaternion, from `absolute_to_relative`), and
  `abs26` (the 26 recorded world positions, metres, rounded to 1e-6 m).
  World orientations were not written; they are reconstructed by chaining
  the relative quaternions (R_child = R_parent R_local,
  p_child = p_parent + R_parent p_local; `xr_hand.kinematics`).
- Quaternion order in the file: keys `qw qx qy qz` per joint; the library
  takes `(qx, qy, qz, qw)` positionally. Both are read by key here.
- Camera space: LeapC desktop mode, right-handed, origin at the module,
  +x along the module to the operator's right, +y up, +z toward the
  operator. Metres in the files, millimetres in the readable tables.
- Selected hand per recording: the metadata `hand` column and the
  `hand_ids` column (space-separated LeapC hand ids). 43 static takes and
  5 transitions also contain the other hand; 44 static and 9 transition
  files contain more than one hand id, of which the selected hand changes
  id once in the static set (p2_s03_power_grasp_finger_side take 1,
  573 to 574, frames 88967 to 89008, camera gap 0.4553 s) and six times in
  the transitions.
- For the selected hand all three clocks are present, strictly increasing
  and never repeated in all 167 recordings. Repeated timestamps exist in the
  files only across the two hands of one tracking frame. The writer clock
  stalls: largest writer delay 838 ms (static) and 865 ms (transitions).
  Confirmed example: p3_circular_precision_thumb_2_fingers take 4, frames
  151749 to 151855, writer span 1.999 s, camera span 1.178 s (a 0.807 s
  writer stall between frames 151852 and 151853 while the camera clock
  advances 10.8 ms).
- Frame-id skips inside the selected hand: 1 in the static set (the
  identity change above) and 6 in the transitions (all at identity
  changes with camera gaps of 0.54 to 2.36 s).
- The repository's existing replay tools: `xr_hand.playback_viewer`
  (matplotlib scrub/play viewer for glove recordings), `xr_hand.viz3d`,
  `xr_hand.kinematics` (FK, quaternion helpers), `xr_hand.joint_frames`
  (per-joint frames, the 24 paper angles, paper-order MCP pair). These are
  reused; no new application framework.

Policies:

- **Hand selection**: lines with `hand_side == hand` and `hand_id in hand_ids`
  and `status != 0`. Everything else is counted, never silently dropped.
  Provenance string names the metadata row the ids came from.
- **Clock**: evaluate all three clocks on the selected frames. Use the first
  of `CLOCK_PREFERENCE` whose report is valid (present on every frame,
  strictly increasing). `timestamp_us` first because it is the camera's own
  acquisition clock in integer microseconds; `capture_time` is the same
  instant placed on the wall clock (rounded to 1e-6 s); `wall_time` is the
  writer and is known to stall. If none is valid, time = frame index times
  the nominal period (1/`framerate` field, else 1/90) and
  `source="frame_index_nominal"`, `fallback=True`. Every fallback is a
  `clock_defect` event and is shown in the viewer HUD and the reports.
- **Discontinuity**: a segment boundary after frame i when
  `hand_id[i+1] != hand_id[i]` (`id_change`) or `t[i+1]-t[i] > gap_threshold_s`
  (`gap`, default 0.05 s, about 4.5 nominal periods). A `frame_id` skip
  inside a segment is a `dropped_frames` event (shown, still interpolated
  because the hand id and the clock are continuous). `status == 0` lines of
  the selected hand are `invalid_status` events and excluded from the pose
  sequence.
- **Interpolation**: never by default. `mode="hold"` shows the latest
  source frame at or before t (`source="held"`, or `"recorded"` when t is
  exactly a source time). `mode="interpolate"` lerps world positions and
  slerps world quaternions between bracketing frames of the same segment;
  across a segment boundary it holds the earlier frame and says so. Outside
  the recording: before the first frame `unavailable`, after the last
  `held`.
- **Reference frames**: `camera` is the recorded motion. `wrist` puts the
  WRIST joint at the origin with its own axes (on the camera that is
  LeapC's forearm orientation, see `xr_hand.joint_frames`); `palm` uses
  the PALM joint's axes at the wrist origin. Both labelled in the viewer.

Declared tolerances (and why):

| Quantity | Tolerance | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| FK position vs recorded `abs26` | 1.0e-6 m per joint (Euclidean) | `abs26` is rounded to 1e-6 m per coordinate (`leap_hand.recorder._round`); worst case sqrt(3)/2 um = 0.87 um; float64 chain error about 1e-15 m |
| matrix chain vs quaternion chain | 1.0e-6 degrees, 1.0e-9 m | same inputs, float64, at most five compositions: expected 1e-12 |
| bone axis vs recorded bone vector | expectation 0.05 degrees, reported | LeapC defines a bone's local -z as prev_joint to next_joint; `abs26` rounding over the shortest bone (about 14 mm) is at most 0.004 degrees |
| capture_time vs timestamp_us intervals | 0.5 ms between consecutive frames, reported | capture_time rounded to 1e-6 s, frame_age_us to 0.1 us, two clocks |
| quaternion norm | 1e-9 of unity | the relative quaternions were normalised when written |

Playback scheduling error is a property of the viewer, not of the data:
during play the displayed frame is the latest source frame at or before
the media time (time-driven, so a late tick shows a later frame rather
than stretching time). Each tick logs intended media time vs the media
time the wall clock had actually reached; stats are in the HUD and in the
timing log written on exit.

`paper3` does not import `replay_core`: the viewer and the batch script
reconstruct the poses with `replay_core.reconstruct` and hand them over.

Relationships implemented, with where they come from in Paper 3
(`paper3 (3).pdf`, pages as printed):

- Table 6, page 15 (circular): per finger i in I, M, R, L:
  MCP_fe ~ 4/3 PIP; PIP ~ 3/2 DIP (also eq. 8, page 14); thumb
  MCP_fe ~ 4/5 IP; TMC_fe ~ 11/10 MCP_fe (unavailable); CMC chain
  I ~ M, M ~ 1/2 R, R ~ 2/3 L (unavailable); M_MCP_aa ~ 1/5 I_MCP_aa;
  R_MCP_aa ~ 1/2 L_MCP_aa.
- Table 7, page 16 (prismatic, thumb/index/middle): T_MCP_fe ~ 6/5 T_IP;
  I_MCP_fe ~ 3/2 I_PIP; M_MCP_fe ~ 3/2 M_PIP; I_PIP ~ 2 I_DIP;
  M_PIP ~ 2 M_DIP. Ring and little keep Table 6 (page 16, section 5.1).
- Equations 3, 4, 5, 7 (pages 13 to 14): conditional on a single finger
  flexing alone; listed, not applied to grasps. Equation 6
  (R_MCP_aa ~ L_MCP_aa, "in most cases"): reported as informational.
- Table 5, page 12: ranges shown as annotations only; it gives no signed
  min/max, so no bound test is built from it.
- Equation 1 and the printed D-H matrix, page 5: the printed matrix has
  `sin(alpha) sin(alpha)` in row 1 column 3 and `-sin(alpha) cos(alpha)` in
  row 2 column 3; the standard D-H transform (Denavit-Hartenberg 1955,
  Spong 2006, cited by the paper) has `sin(alpha) sin(theta)` and
  `-sin(alpha) cos(theta)`. The test shows the printed rotation block is
  not orthogonal for generic angles and the corrected one is; the corrected
  matrix is used, and this is the one correction made.
- Equations 9 to 11, pages 16 to 17: the fingertip relative error of the
  simplified descriptions. Implemented with the 9-DoF description
  (page 15), recorded bone lengths, CMC set to zero on both sides
  (unavailable), thumb unavailable (TMC pair missing). Reported as a
  research-model number, separate from fidelity.

Class mapping: `p3_circular_*` -> circular, `p3_prismatic_*` -> prismatic
(recorded under Paper 3's own labels). `p1_*` and `p2_*` -> unmapped: they
were recorded under the Paper 1 and Paper 2 taxonomies and no object-shape
record supports a Cutkosky circular/prismatic assignment. Unmapped grasps
get only eq. 8 (PIP ~ 3/2 DIP, "the most accepted intra-finger constraint",
page 14), flagged general.

MCP flexion for the relationships uses the paper-order pair
(`joint_frames.paper_order_angles`, abduction first as in the paper's D-H
chain, Tables 2 and 3); the main XYZ-order value is reported beside it.
Anatomical alignment of either with a Cyberglove reading is approximate.

## Corrections after measurement (2026-10-23, after the batch run)

The table above is kept as pre-registered. Two of its premises were wrong
and are corrected in `docs/replay_fidelity.md` and in the `why` text of
`replay_core.TOLERANCES`; no declared value changed:

- The shortest recorded bone is 7.36 mm (INDEX_DISTAL, p1_lateral take 2),
  not about 14 mm, so `abs26` rounding allows up to 0.0135 degrees of
  bone-direction angle there, not 0.004. The measured maximum is 0.0108
  degrees, under the declared 0.05 degree expectation.
- Table 5's second column is "Hyperextension", not abduction.

Also found after the plan: equations 6 and 7 are printed on page 13 (only
Figure 8 is on page 14); eq. 8 duplicates Table 6's PIP/DIP row for
circular grasps and is marked `duplicate_of` in the outputs; eq. 9 as
printed divides by |rp| and can exceed 100 % when the rebuilt finger curls
toward its base.

## Acceptance checks

See `GATES_replay.md`. In short: unit tests per leaf (rigid transforms and
quaternion composition against a hand-written Rodrigues reference, left and
right synthetic hands, exact replay at source frames, timestamp-based play
and speed, repeated/nonmonotonic/missing clocks, gaps and identity changes,
Paper 3 coefficients and availability, the D-H correction); the full
existing suite; the batch run over all 167 recordings with the declared
tolerances met and the counts reported; the four named scenarios
reproduced with their known facts; headless screenshots of the viewer in
its states; an interactive launch; the dataset's SHA-256 unchanged against
the forensic baseline.
