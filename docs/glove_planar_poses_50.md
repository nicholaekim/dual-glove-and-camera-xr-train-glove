# 50 single-plane poses for the XR glove, with reference joint ranges

Written 7 Nov 2026 by `scripts/leap/glove_planar_poses.py`; the same data is `protocols/glove_planar_poses_50.csv`, and `protocols/glove_planar_poses_50.json` is the recorder's pose list. Gates: `GATES_glove_planar_poses.md`.

## What this is

Fifty static hand poses that are not in the database yet, chosen so the StretchSense XR glove can record them, each with an approximate reference range for every angle the glove reports: finger MCP flexion, PIP and DIP, thumb MCP and IP. The purpose is the test a colleague proposed: record new poses with the glove and see whether the measured angles satisfy the constraint tables compiled on 7 Nov 2026 (Dragusanu 2025 Table I, Isaac 2021 Table 1, Ingram 2008 Table 1, Chen 2013 Table 6 which is Cobos 2009 Table 5, Jia 2021 Table 1, and the MyoHand model). A range is what a held pose should look like, not a pass/fail limit. Each pose also says which constraint it exercises.

## Why one plane

The glove senses flexion: MCP and PIP from its sensors, DIP from its hand model, and the thumb as a coupled estimate. It has no sensor for finger spread (the pilot export shows a fixed splay) and none for the wrist, and its flexion never reads below 0, so it cannot show hyperextension. So every pose here keeps the fingers side by side, the wrist straight and the thumb in its own bending plane, and uses no hyperextension state: each digit moves only in its own flexion-extension plane: fingers side by side (spread together), wrist neutral, thumb bent or straight in its own plane. A pose that needed spread, wrist motion or thumb opposition would be measured by the camera only, which is the camera catalogue's job (`docs/pose_catalogue_100.md`).

## How to read the ranges

Every pose is written as digit states and every state has one template range, imported from the camera catalogue so the two lists agree. Angles are the repo's (`docs/joint_frames.md`): flexion positive toward the palm. Spread is `together` and the wrist `neutral` throughout, so their columns are constant and are not repeated in the table (they are in the CSV). The thumb's TMC pair is not comparable across sources (`docs/tmc_estimate.md`), so the thumb carries its MCP and IP ranges and the TMC in words.

Finger flexion templates (MCP, PIP, DIP, degrees):

| state | MCP flexion | PIP | DIP | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ext | -10 to 15 | 0 to 10 | 0 to 10 | straight |
| quarter | 15 to 35 | 15 to 35 | 5 to 20 | a quarter curled |
| half | 35 to 60 | 35 to 65 | 15 to 45 | half curled |
| flex | 70 to 90 | 85 to 110 | 50 to 90 | fully curled |
| hook | 0 to 20 | 70 to 100 | 40 to 80 | MCP straight, PIP and DIP bent (claw) |
| tabletop | 70 to 90 | 0 to 15 | 0 to 10 | MCP bent 90, PIP and DIP straight |
| tipcurl | 0 to 15 | 30 to 60 | 30 to 60 | MCP straight, PIP and DIP half bent |

Thumb states (thumb MCP flexion, thumb IP flexion; the TMC in words, for the glove):

| state | thumb MCP | thumb IP | TMC |
|---|---|---|---|
| up | -10 to 15 | -10 to 15 | straight, in the palm plane, pointing along the radial side |
| along | 0 to 20 | 0 to 20 | adducted, lying along the index metacarpal |
| across | 20 to 50 | 20 to 60 | half flexed across the palm, over the fingers |
| tucked | 30 to 55 | 40 to 80 | fully flexed into the palm, under or against the fingers |
| ip_bent | -10 to 15 | 40 to 80 | straight at the MCP, only the tip joint bent |
| mcp_bent | 30 to 55 | -10 to 10 | bent at the MCP only, tip joint straight |

Every template lies inside the literature envelope of the camera catalogue (`docs/pose_catalogue_100.md`, table "envelope"): MCP flexion -40 to 90, PIP -5 to 135, DIP -30 to 90, thumb MCP -30 to 80, thumb IP -45 to 90. Two numbers matter for the glove in particular: MyoHand caps PIP at 90 where Chen allows 110 to 135, and the glove's own export topped out at 90 or 100 degrees of PIP in the pilot fist, so a full curl may read a ceiling rather than the finger.

## What is excluded as already in the database

The ten paired poses of 2026-11-05 (open palm, fist, index point, thumbs up, peace, pinch, finger spread, thumb opposition, crossed fingers, bent wrist), the July seven, the 23 mimed grasps of Set A, every step of the seven Set C sequences, and all 100 poses of the Leap (camera) catalogue of 7 Nov 2026. The exclusion is by **finger shape**: the four finger states alone, whatever the thumb, the spread or the wrist. A glove pose whose four fingers match a Leap pose or a recorded pose is left out even when its thumb or spread differs, because the glove does not sense spread or the wrist and its thumb is its weakest estimate, so to the glove it would be the same pose. That rules out 77 finger shapes (57 from the Leap list, 34 from the recorded poses, some shared), among them every whole-hand shape (all four fingers straight, quarter, half, tabletop, tip curl, hook or fist) and every single finger at tabletop, hook, half or quarter. No two of the 50 share a finger shape with each other either. `tests/test_glove_planar_poses.py` checks all of it. The professor's 102 tracker frames carry no pose names and are not matched.

## Recording setup

Every pose: gloves on, no camera: XR Train open and streaming, the hand held still in the pose for the 5 s of the take. Setup for every step: gloves on, no camera. No objects. The glove recording uses no camera at all: the Leap poses are one set, recorded with the camera, and these are a separate, different set, recorded with the glove alone. One take is one held pose, three takes per pose, left hand. The glove note in the table says what the glove cannot sense in that pose. One launcher records the recording set with the glove-only recorder:

    launchers\record_glove_poses.cmd

It prints the setup, waits for a key, and runs:

    .venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\glove\record_poses.py --hand left --pose-list protocols\glove_planar_poses_recording_set.json --takes 3 --first-take auto --duration 5 --prep 5 --hz 0 --out-dir recordings\glove_planar_left --app-screenshot "XR Train"

The recorder takes the 10 poses of the recording set (below) from `protocols/glove_planar_poses_recording_set.json` in this order (ps01,pd01,pf07,pr07,pc01,pc05,pm01,pt01,pt02,pt03) and, for each, prints the id with its name and description, counts down 5 s (beeps on 3, 2, 1) and records 5 s, keeping every frame of the left glove. To carry on after a stop, give the launcher the pose to start at, for example `launchers\record_glove_poses.cmd pc01`; takes are numbered after the ones already on disk.

**A screenshot of XR Train for every take.** XR Train must be open with its window not minimised (it may sit behind other windows). At the midpoint of every take's 5 s, the recorder saves a picture of the XR Train window to `recordings\glove_planar_left\xr_train\<take>.png`, named like the take, and the take's `meta.json` says where it is, when and how it was captured (`app_screenshot*` keys), beside the pose, its name and description, the hand, the frame count and the take's start and end times. If a picture cannot be taken the take is still kept and its meta says why; the recorder refuses to start at all if the window cannot be captured, and the session's last lines count how many takes have a picture. Gates: `GATES_glove_recording.md`.

`protocols/glove_planar_poses_50.json` holds all 50 items in the same pose-list format (id, label, shape), for recording the others later with the same recorder.
