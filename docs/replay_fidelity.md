# Replay fidelity: method, tolerances and measured results

Measured on 2026-10-23 by `scripts/leap/replay_verify.py` over every
recording of `grasp set 2026-10-08 organized` (115 static takes, 52
transitions). Every number on this page comes from that run. The
machine-readable results are in `results\replay_fidelity\` (git-ignored,
regenerated by the command in section 1). The plan is `docs/replay_plan.md`;
the acceptance checks are in `GATES_replay.md` (R6, R7 and R9 are this run and
its two checks).

Two claims are kept apart, as in the plan:

1. **Replay fidelity.** The replay shows what the tracker recorded. This is
   checked numerically against declared tolerances, and each recording gets
   a verdict (sections 2 to 7).
2. **Paper 3 agreement.** How the recorded angles relate to the intra-finger
   relationships of Cobos et al. 2009. This is a research comparison. The
   paper supplies no threshold, so there is no verdict (section 8).

Neither claim is physical accuracy against the person's hand. That would
need a reference measurement this dataset does not have.

## 2. Method: what is compared with what

| Comparison | Against | Independent? | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint positions rebuilt by chaining the 26 parent-relative `joints` through `xr_hand.kinematics.forward_kinematics_full`, the chain every tool in the repository draws hands with | the recorded `abs26` world positions, which the recorder wrote next to the chain | yes: `abs26` never passes through the chain code | gated, 1e-6 m per joint |
| Rebuilt axis of each of the 19 bones (local -z of the chained world rotation, LeapC's prev_joint to next_joint direction) | the recorded bone vector `abs26[next] - abs26[joint]` | yes: orientation comes from the quaternions and direction from the positions, which were written separately | reported, expectation 0.05 deg |
| Matrix chain | quaternion-only chain (`reconstruct_quaternion_chain`) | no: two code paths over the same data, a cross-check of the implementation, not a measurement | gated, 1e-6 deg and 1e-9 m |
| `capture_time` intervals | `timestamp_us` intervals | two recorded clocks of one device pipeline | reported, 0.5 ms |
| Norm of each relative quaternion | 1 | n/a | reported, 1e-9 |

Some things cannot be checked from this dataset at all:

- **World orientations as the tracker reported them.** The recorder wrote
  only the parent-relative quaternions. A bone's roll about its own axis,
  and the WRIST and PALM orientations, are rebuilt but have no recorded
  reference.
- **Physical accuracy against the hand.**

A recording is within tolerance when three numbers are at or under their
limits: the position residual, the chain angle and the chain position. A
recording without `abs26` could not be declared within tolerance, but every
selected frame here has `abs26`.

**Verdict:** 167 of 167 recordings are within the three gated tolerances.

**Reading the position residual.** It matches the rounding of `abs26`
alone:

- The worst value, 0.8602 um, is under the rounding bound sqrt(3)/2 um =
  0.8660 um.
- The mean over all 1,864,434 joint residuals (71,709 frames x 26 joints) is
  0.4802 um. Uniform rounding to 1 um predicts 0.4803 um, the mean distance
  from the centre of a 1 um cube to a uniform point in it (0.48030 +- 0.00004
  um by Monte Carlo).

The chain cross-check differs at 1e-14 deg and 1e-16 m, about seven orders
of magnitude below its gates.

## 5. Clock policy

**Preference order:** `timestamp_us`, then `capture_time`, then `wall_time`,
then frame index x nominal period. A clock is *valid* when it is present on
every selected frame and strictly increasing, and the first valid clock is
used. Frames are never reordered to suit a clock.

**Why `timestamp_us` comes first.** Each clock records something different:

- `timestamp_us` is the camera's own acquisition clock (the LeapC device
  clock) in integer microseconds.
- `capture_time` is the same instant placed on the wall clock, rounded to
  1e-6 s.
- `wall_time` is when the writer reached the line, and it stalls (below).

**What a fallback looks like.** When the preferred clock is not valid:

- `Timeline.source` names the clock used and `fallback` is true.
- `reason` lists each rejected clock with its defect counts, for example
  `timestamp_us rejected (missing 0, repeated 1, nonmonotonic 0 of 5 frames);
  capture_time used: present on all 5 frames, strictly increasing`.
- A `clock_defect` event marks the first defective frame on the timeline.
- It shows in the viewer HUD and in `fidelity_per_recording.csv` (columns
  `clock_source`, `clock_fallback` and `n_clock_defect`).

If no clock is valid, time is frame index x 1/`framerate` (the lines'
median, else 1/90 s), and the source is `frame_index_nominal`. The core's
tests exercise repeated, nonmonotonic and missing clocks. None of this occurs
in this dataset: 167 of 167 recordings use `timestamp_us`, with no fallback
and no `clock_defect` event.

**How closely `capture_time` and `timestamp_us` agree.** The comparison is
|(capture_time[i+1] - capture_time[i]) - (timestamp_us[i+1] -
timestamp_us[i]) / 1e6| over consecutive selected frames:

| static | 51,505 | 0.0019 ms | 0.0120 ms | 0.184 ms |
| transitions | 20,037 | 0.0019 ms | 0.0140 ms | 0.326 ms (p2_s07_directional_power_side take 4) |

## 6. Discontinuity policy

**Where a segment ends.** A segment boundary falls after frame i in either
of two cases:

- `hand_id[i+1] != hand_id[i]` (`id_change`). The tracker lost the hand, and
  the new id may not even be the same hand.
- `t[i+1] - t[i] > 0.05 s` on the camera clock (`gap`). 0.05 s is about 4.5
  nominal periods at 90 Hz.

A boundary that is both gives both events. The frame ids skipped there are
counted in the events' details, not as a third event.

**What happens at a boundary:**

- **No interpolation across it.** `pose_at(..., "interpolate")` lerps world
  positions and slerps world quaternions only inside one segment. Across a
  boundary it holds the earlier frame and says so in its note.
- **Dropped frames.** A frame-id skip with the same hand id and a continuous
  clock is a `dropped_frames` event. It is shown on the timeline and still
  interpolated.
- **Status-0 lines.** A status-0 line of the selected hand is excluded from
  the pose sequence and marked as `invalid_status`.

Measured: 7 boundaries (section 4), every one both an `id_change` and a
`gap`. There are no `dropped_frames` and no `invalid_status` events.

**The identity-change example: p2_s03_power_grasp_finger_side take 1.**

- **Two segments.** Frames 0 to 60 have hand id 573 (frame ids 88907 to
  88967, 61 frames). Frames 61 to 408 have hand id 574 (frame ids 89008 to
  89355, 348 frames).
- **The boundary.** The camera clock steps 0.455302 s across it, and 40
  frame ids are skipped.
- **The midpoint pose.** In interpolate mode, at the midpoint between frames
  88967 and 89008 (t = 0.8944 s), `pose_at` returns `source = "held"` and
  shows frame 88967. Its note reads: "discontinuity (id_change, gap) between
  frame_id 88967 and 89008: holding the earlier frame, not interpolated".

The report is `scenarios\identity_change.json`.

## 7. Playback scheduling and the scenario reports

Playback scheduling error belongs to the viewer and the machine, not to the
data, and it is measured separately by the viewer's tick log
(`ReplayViewer.write_timing_log`; the log's `what_is_logged` field defines
each number). Playback is time-driven: at every tick the displayed frame is
the latest source frame at or before the media time, so a late tick shows a
later frame instead of stretching time, and the media clock is never
affected by draw time. Measured on 2026-10-23 on this machine (i7-13620H,
TkAgg, 15.5 x 8.6 inch window, overlay and axes off, no other Python
process running), `scripts\leap\replay_view.py --grasp p3_circular_power
--take 1 --duration 8`:

| Speed | Ticks in 8 s | Lateness mean / p95 / max (media ms) | Source frames skipped between displayed frames |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1x | 56 | 74.8 / 95.7 / 128.2 | 392 of the 449 frames passed |
| 0.25x | 84 | 18.6 / 23.4 / 30.4 | 85 |

So at 1x this matplotlib viewer draws about 7 frames per second of a 90 Hz
recording: the pose shown is always a true source frame at the right media
time, but most frames are not drawn at full speed. At 0.25x about one frame
in two is drawn. Frame stepping, event jumps and scrubbing land on exact
source frames (`displayed pose: recorded` in the HUD) and do not depend on
the tick rate. Logs: `results\replay_fidelity\timing_tk_p3_circular_power_take1_speed1.json`
and `..._speed0.25.json`; a driven interactive session that exercised every
control on TkAgg while the batch run was competing for CPU is in
`results\replay_fidelity\interactive_tk\` (81 ticks, mean 78.9 ms, p95
158.0 ms, max 495.0 ms over four play runs at 1x, 2x and 0.5x).

## 8. Paper 3 comparison (research, no verdict)

Source: Cobos et al. 2009, "Human Hand Descriptions and Gesture Recognition
for Object Manipulation" (`paper3 (3).pdf`). Pages are the printed page
numbers. The implementation is `leap_hand.paper3`. Its angles are those of
`xr_hand.joint_frames`: flexion positive toward the palm and abduction
positive toward the thumb, on both hands.

For each relation `lhs ~ coeff x rhs` the residual is `lhs - coeff x rhs`
in degrees, frame by frame. The statistics reported are:

- n, mean, MAE, RMSE and percentiles of the residual;
- the empirical least-squares line `lhs = slope x rhs + intercept`, whose
  slope can be read against the paper's coefficient;
- Pearson r.

**Pooled** means one series per class: every frame of every recording of
that class concatenated. Each frame counts once, so a recording mean is
never averaged with another. Static takes give two pools: all frames, and
hold frames (the metadata static interval). Transitions give one pool, all
frames.

### Assumptions and corrections disclosed

- **MCP order.** The finger MCP relations use the paper-order MCP pair:
  abduction first, as the paper's D-H chain turns the joints (Table 2, p.5;
  `joint_frames.paper_order_angles`). The main XYZ-order reading is in the
  `*_main` columns. For flexion the two readings differ by at most 1.1 deg
  in the pooled means. For the abduction relations of prismatic grasps they
  differ more:

  | Class (static, all frames) | Relation | mean / RMSE / slope / r (paper order) | mean / RMSE / slope / r (main order) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | circular | I_MCP_fe ~ 4/3 I_PIP | -34.44 / 42.44 / 0.423 / 0.604 | -34.08 / 42.24 / 0.428 / 0.595 |
  | circular | M_MCP_aa ~ 1/5 I_MCP_aa | -4.62 / 7.11 / 0.192 / 0.316 | -4.06 / 6.35 / 0.162 / 0.233 |
  | prismatic | M_MCP_aa ~ 1/5 I_MCP_aa | -12.67 / 14.41 / 0.517 / 0.765 | -6.37 / 7.19 / 0.264 / 0.506 |
  | prismatic | R_MCP_aa ~ 1/2 L_MCP_aa | -6.04 / 10.28 / 0.571 / 0.577 | -3.49 / 6.86 / 0.450 / 0.421 |

  How closely either reading lines up anatomically with the paper's
  Cyberglove angles is approximate.
- **Six coordinates are not given by the camera.** These are T_TMC_fe,
  T_TMC_aa, I_CMC, M_CMC, R_CMC and L_CMC (`joint_frames.CAMERA_NOT_GIVEN`):
  LeapC's palm is rigid, and the thumb's TMC pair is taken about other axes
  than the paper's. They are NaN in every frame by construction, and the
  circular and prismatic scenario reports confirm it on their recordings.
  The four relations that need them (T_TMC_fe ~ 11/10 T_MCP_fe and
  the CMC chain) are `unavailable`, with no numbers.
- **The D-H correction (p.5)** is the one correction made to the paper. The
  printed matrix has sin(alpha) sin(alpha) in row 1 column 3 and
  -sin(alpha) cos(alpha) in row 2 column 3. The standard transform the paper
  cites (Denavit and Hartenberg 1955; Spong et al. 2006) has
  sin(alpha) sin(theta) and -sin(alpha) cos(theta). The printed rotation
  block is not orthogonal for generic angles. The standard matrix is used,
  and `paper3.dh_as_printed` keeps the misprint for the record.
- **Eq. 8 duplicates Table 6 for circular grasps.** Both rows are reported.
  The eq. 8 row carries `duplicate_of` = the Table 6 id (112 such rows: 28
  circular recordings x 4 fingers) and should be counted once.
- **The eq. 9 denominator.** Eq. 9 divides by |rp| as printed. The
  wrist-to-finger-base offset u_i of eq. 1 has no value in the paper. Tips
  are therefore relative to each finger's base frame, and |rp| is shorter
  than the paper's wrist-relative distance. Where the rebuilt finger curls
  back toward its base, |rp| is small and the per-frame percentage passes
  100 %. It is reported as printed.

   `joint_frames`, not a Cyberglove's.
5. **MCP residuals.** The MCP residuals above are large. The rebuilt fingers

## 9. Known limitations

- **A correction to the plan's premise for the bone-direction expectation.**
  The plan and the justification text in `replay_core.TOLERANCES` put the
  shortest bone at about 14 mm, giving a rounding effect of at most 0.004
  deg. The measured figures are smaller:

  | Bone | Recording | Shortest length (mean) | Rounding bound atan(sqrt(3) um / L) |
  |---|---|---|---:|
  | INDEX_DISTAL, the shortest in any frame | static p1_lateral take 2 | 7.36 mm (mean 7.81 mm) | 0.0135 deg |
  | LITTLE_DISTAL, where the worst angle occurs | p3_circular_precision_thumb_2_fingers take 2 | 7.63 mm (mean 7.95 mm) | 0.0130 deg |

  Rounding each end of a bone to 1e-6 m moves the bone vector by at most
  sqrt(3) um, which gives the bound in the last column. The measured
  maximum, 0.0108 deg, is under both bounds. The declared 0.05 deg
  expectation holds, and its value is unaffected. Only the 14 mm / 0.004
  deg wording in `TOLERANCES['bone_direction_deg_expectation']['why']` is
  out of date.
- **Orientation is only partly verifiable.** No world orientation was
  recorded. Orientation is checked through the 19 bone directions (and the
  chain cross-check, which is not independent). A bone's roll about its own
  axis, and the WRIST and PALM orientations, have no recorded reference.
- **Physical accuracy is not assessable.** Everything here is agreement with
  the recording, not with the hand.
- **Bone lengths are the tracker's.** They change within a recording, up to
  an SD of 3.61 mm for THUMB_METACARPAL in p3_circular_precision_thumb_1_finger
  take 1. The replay reproduces that, because it is what was recorded.
- **The writer clock.** It stalls (section 5), and the metadata static
  intervals were cut on it. In p3_circular_precision_thumb_2_fingers take 4
  the declared 2 s interval covers 1.178 s of camera time. Replay uses the
  camera clock, so playback is not affected. Durations read from the
  metadata are.
- **The clock agreement check is internal.** It compares two clocks of one
  device pipeline. It does not test the camera clock against an external
  reference.
- **Pooled statistics weight frames, not recordings.** A longer recording
  weighs more. The per-recording rows are in `paper3_residuals.csv`.
- **The Paper 3 layer is research context.** Class mapping covers the p3_*
  grasps only. Angle conventions against the Cyberglove are approximate. The
  fingertip error carries the five caveats of section 8. No relation has a
  pass/fail threshold.
- **Playback scheduling.** The matplotlib viewer draws about 7 frames per
  second at 1x on this machine (section 7), so full-speed play shows
  one source frame in about seven. The media clock and the frame shown
  at any instant stay correct; stepping shows every frame.
